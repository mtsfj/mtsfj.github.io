This is a pixel-art painting of a Japanese street at dusk, enlarged 2 times (each painting pixel is a 2 x 2 block). Left of the middle, under the pink wooden wall with the big screen, is a small sweets shop (a dagashi-ya) with a dark signboard above its window, the pale word だがし on it. Repaint ONLY that signboard; everything else stays exactly as it is.

It must look painted by the same artist, the same day, as the other signs in this picture, and read as well at its small size as the red おでん sign on the roof on the right: the same pixel-painting style (simple bold shapes, soft painterly shading, no outlines, no small text, no logos, no photographic detail), the same palette and value range, the same soft glow as the おでん sign and lantern.

A long horizontal lit sign box on the shop's dark fascia, an old one: its face of milky white acrylic lit warm from inside, painted softly, brightest in its middle and a little dimmer and warmer towards its ends, with a faint uneven tone where the lamps behind it sit; a dark wooden frame round it, its lower edge dusty, one corner of the frame slightly chipped, a thin dark streak of old rain stain down its face. The word だがし across it in thick, simple, deep red-brown letters with soft brush ends, well spaced and centred, easy to read. Its warm glow spills softly on the dark fascia right round its frame and faintly on the wall's boards just above it. It lies on the wall in the wall's perspective: its top and bottom edges slant gently down to the right like the wall's board lines, the letters following that slant. Same size and place as the old board.
