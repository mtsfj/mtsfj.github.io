Pinductor

Learning POMDP world models from observations with language-model priors.

Valentin Six*, Frederik Panse*, Mathis Fajeau*, Lancelot Da Costa*, Mridul Sharma†, Alfonso Amayuelas†, Tim Z. Xiao, David Hyland, Philipp Hennig, Bernhard Schölkopf
* equal first authors · † equal second authors · MPI for Intelligent Systems, IRIIS, UC Santa Barbara, University of Tübingen, University of Oxford, ELLIS Institute Tübingen
arXiv v1 · 13 May 2026 alphaXiv Public code

An agent dropped into a MiniGrid room sees a 3×3 window of the world and nothing else. Pinductor asks a language model to write the room's rules as Python (initial state, transition, observation, reward), then checks each candidate program the only way a partially observed agent can: by running a particle filter under the program's own dynamics and asking whether its imagined observations explain the real ones. The score, the diagnostics it produces, and the committee disagreement among candidates are fed back to the model for repair. No hidden state is ever used for learning. The result matches POMDP Coder, which trains on privileged ground-truth states, and clears tabular, recurrent-RL and world-model baselines on five MiniGrid tasks, a partially observed Blocks World and PopGym MineSweeper.

1 · The setting

An agent that sees three cells

Whether it navigates a building, operates a robot or plays a game, an agent acting in a new environment first needs an internal model of how that environment responds to its actions. The paper studies the hardest version of that problem in a controlled setting: a MiniGrid room where the agent perceives only the 3×3 window in front of it (highlighted in blue), its compass direction, and whatever it is carrying. Not its position. Not the map. Not the location of the lava.

The room on the left is the paper's Lava task, drawn from the released demonstration file: a 10×10 grid, a column of lava with a single gap, the goal in the far corner. The column and the gap move between episodes, so nothing about the layout can be memorised.

The question the paper asks: can a language model's prior knowledge replace the state supervision that earlier program-induction methods relied on, so the agent can still write down an executable model of this world from its own partial observations?

2 · Background

The POMDP, and what stays hidden

A partially observable Markov decision process is a tuple of states, actions, observations, a transition model, an observation model, a reward and an initial-state distribution. The agent never sees the state s. It keeps a belief Q, a distribution over states, and updates it by Bayes' rule after every action and observation:

eq. 2Q'(s') ∝ O(s', a, o) · Σ_s T(s, a, s') Q(s)

That update needs T, O and ρ₀. In the paper's setting none of them is given; they must be learned from trajectories that contain observations, actions, rewards and termination flags only. Rewards and termination are outcomes of the transition, not perceptual fields: the agent receives them each step but the observation program is never asked to reproduce them.

The panel in the stage's corner prints the current observation exactly as the code does: a 3×3 integer image (2 wall, 1 empty, 11 lava, 10 goal, 12 the agent's own cell), the direction, and the carried object.

3 · Environments

Five gridworlds, one observation interface

The arXiv paper evaluates on five deterministic MiniGrid tasks that vary in what the model has to get right. Each button loads a real initial state from the released N = 10 demonstration files and replays that demonstration; the switcher also carries the three stochastic variants of Appendix D.1 and the two non-grid suites of the camera-ready version.

  • Empty: an empty room, the sanity check; every method except random solves it.
  • Corners: the goal sits in a random corner of a 10×10 room. With a 3×3 view the room is almost rotationally symmetric, so the difficulty is disambiguating where you are.
  • Lava: a terminal hazard. A model that does not encode lava as terminating produces a planner that takes the shortcut and dies. Reward is sparse, so the model must combine the description and the trajectories to learn what lava means.
  • Four Rooms: long-horizon navigation through narrow doorways toward a goal the agent cannot see; each doorway choice is a near-blind decision under the belief over goal location. (The released code uses a 15×15 layout.)
  • Unlock: two rooms separated by a locked door. Find the key, pick it up, toggle the door, traverse, reach the goal. The model must encode the carrying channel and the door-state transition from observation traces alone.

Two suites are not grids. In Blocks World six numbered blocks are dealt afresh over three columns each episode and the gripper, which does not know its own column, sees only the stack beneath it and the block in its hand; the target tower is drawn faintly on the left column, and the replayed demonstration is one of the fifteen breadth-first-optimal solutions the language model was shown. In PopGym MineSweeper a click returns the number of mines adjacent to that cell; the replays are the fifty rollouts the paper used as demonstrations, of which twenty-three end on a mine and twenty-five clear the board; the default replay is a cleared board. Switch to the true-state view to see where the mines were. These two suites matter later, when the question becomes whether the language model learns from data or recalls MiniGrid.

4 · Prior work

What earlier methods quietly assumed

WorldCoder, GIF-MCTS and most LLM program-synthesis methods assume the world is fully observable. The closest predecessor, POMDP Coder (Curtis et al., CoRL 2025), does learn POMDP programs, but after every episode it is handed the ground-truth states it could not observe at decision time, and its refinement prompts compare predicted states with recorded states.

Toggle the two. POMDP Coder learns from the whole grid plus the true pose at every step. Pinductor learns from the cells the agent actually saw, strictly less information. In robots operating in cluttered spaces or agents in imperfect-information games, neither online nor post-hoc state access exists, so a method that needs it cannot be applied at all.

Both methods otherwise share the pipeline, the language model (Qwen 3.6 Plus), the belief-space planner and the evaluation seeds. The one difference the paper isolates is where the learning signal comes from: hidden states, or particle-filtered beliefs.

5 · Method, step 1

The language model proposes the world as code

The prompt contains a natural-language environment description, the ten state-free trajectories, and a code API describing the state, action and observation types. It asks for one Python program implementing four functions: initial_func, transition_func, observation_func, reward_func. Pinductor makes a single joint call per candidate; POMDP Coder makes four per-component calls.

Below are four candidate hypotheses for Lava. They are hand-written stand-ins shaped like the model outputs reproduced in the paper's Appendix I, and they are the programs every interactive panel on this page executes. The stage shows K = 10 states sampled from each candidate's initial-state distribution, the world it imagines before seeing anything.


  


  

    
6 · Method, step 2

    
Score by filtering, not by state labels

    
For each candidate m, Pinductor runs a particle filter under the candidate's own dynamics along a recorded trajectory. Particles start from ρ₀ᵐ, are pushed through Tᵐ under the recorded actions, and each predicts an observation with the candidate's observation program. Because that program is usually deterministic, its prediction is softened into a kernel likelihood through an observation distance:

    
eq. 5, 11d_obs(o', o) = d_grid(g', g) + 1[θ' ≠ θ] + 1[c' ≠ c]
d_grid = −log( ε + (1−ε) · (matching visible cells / visible cells)³ )

    
eq. 6, 8Oᵐ(o | s', a) ∝ exp( −d_obs(oᵐ(s', a), o) / κ )
L(Pᵐ; D) = Σ_τ Σ_t E_{s ~ Q_{t+1}ᵐ} [ log Oᵐ(o_{t+1} | s, a_t) ]

    
The belief Q is resampled to integer particle counts after every observation. When the total post-observation weight drops below K, fresh particles are drawn from ρ₀ᵐ, rolled through the whole action history and kept with probability proportional to their cumulative kernel likelihood (rejuvenation). The score is the filtered expected log-likelihood, up to constants the negative expected observation energy under the belief the candidate itself induces.

7 · Method, step 3

    
Execution becomes debugging feedback

    
The next prompt does not carry only a scalar score. It summarises where the candidate failed: execution errors, trajectory segments with large observation distance, reward and termination mismatches, and the particle filter's health. The block below is assembled live from the filter lab's current state, in the format of the refinement prompt reproduced in Appendix I.

    

    
The prompt also carries a query-by-committee signal. The true transition is unavailable, so the transition programs proposed so far act as a committee: for a belief particle s and an action a, each votes for a next state, and disagreement is the normalised vote entropy

    
eq. 9VE(s, a) = −(1 / log|C|) · Σ_y (V_{s,a}(y) / |C|) · log(V_{s,a}(y) / |C|)

    
computed on belief particles visited during filtering, for realised and counterfactual actions. Contexts with high entropy are shown to the model together with the observation and the action, so it sees where the current family of programs is unsure. The signal is diagnostic only; it never enters the selection objective.

8 · Method, step 4

    
Refinement as a tree, not a chain

    
Refinement-by-execution repeats proposal, scoring and feedback over J rounds. Each round picks one existing candidate as the parent, asks the language model for M = 5 revised programs, scores them and adds them to a persistent pool. The parent is chosen with UCB1, so high-scoring candidates are refined more often but less-explored branches keep an exploration bonus:

    
eq. 17UCB(p) = S̄(p) + c_ucb · sqrt( log n_parent(p) / (n_p + 1) )      c_ucb = 1

    
The smoothed denominator keeps the bonus finite for unexpanded nodes, so a fresh node cannot dominate on the bonus alone. Programs that crash during filtering receive −∞, stay in the pool for their diagnostics, and can never be selected. After all rounds the final program is sampled from the near-best set, every candidate within one empirical standard deviation of the best score, with a softmax at temperature T = 0.1:

    
eq. 18, 19N = { j : S_j ≥ S* − std({S_j'}) }      Pr(m* = m_j) ∝ exp(S_j / T),  j ∈ N

9 · Method, step 5

    
Plan in belief space with the program you chose

    
The selected program is used directly by a belief-space planner, the determinized A* of POMDP Coder (PO-DA*). Nodes are beliefs; an edge applies an action and branches on the predicted observation. Each branch costs

    
g' = g − E[r] − λ · log p(o) + action cost,      priority = g' + entropy_coeff · H(Q')

    
with a budget of 5,000 belief expansions. The entropy term is the one hyperparameter where the two LLM methods differ: 1.0 for Pinductor, 0 for POMDP Coder, because tuning it helped Pinductor and did nothing for the baseline. During the episode the belief is tracked with the same distance kernel used for scoring, so the object the planner consumes is the object the score evaluated.

10 · Method, step 6

    
Close the loop

    
After each evaluation episode the new trajectory is appended to the replay buffer and a fresh REx round runs online (five attempts), so the program keeps improving from the agent's own experience. The protocol behind every number in the next section: ten offline demonstrations, three evaluation episodes per run seed, ten seeds, 95% bootstrap intervals over seed-level means.

    
The whole loop runs on a CPU workstation; the language model is reached through OpenRouter. The cost is real: median wall-clock per Lava run is 33 minutes for Pinductor against 17 for POMDP Coder, and Pinductor's joint prompts consume roughly three times the tokens.

12 · Limitations and review

  
Where the paper is thin, in the reviewers' words and the authors'

  
The conclusions section names four limitations: a hand-designed observation distance, manually collected demonstrations, no cross-environment transfer, and MiniGrid only. The NeurIPS meta-review (three reviewers, scores 4 / 3 / 2) sharpened them into seven points. Each card pairs the point with what the rebuttal added; green cards were answered with new experiments now in the code release.

  

    
Meta-review 1
Stronger non-LLM baselines
Recurrent model-free RL, DVRL, VRM, predictive-state or Bayes-adaptive estimators were missing; the tabular baseline was weak.
Added: PPO-GRU, DreamerV3 online and demonstration-seeded, a direct-LLM policy in both observability regimes, an exact optimal reference, and a tabular arm at 1,000 demonstrations. On MiniGrid demonstration-seeded DreamerV3 competes; on Blocks every RL arm is at zero.

    
Meta-review 2
Prior recall versus learning
MiniGrid is in every pretraining corpus. Does the belief-based signal ever correct the prior, or is the model reciting?
Added: the Blocks-rand6 domain with withheld conventions, the transition-exactness audit, the wrap-around case study where data contradicted the prior and the repair followed, and prompt-scaffold ablations. The initial-state distribution is stated verbatim in the prompt, so it certifies faithful implementation, not learning; learning claims rest on the withheld transition rules.

    
Meta-review 3
Isolate the effect of removing state supervision
The comparison to POMDP Coder also changes prompting (one joint call versus four), scoring, feedback and planner settings.
Partly open. The paper states each difference and matches hyperparameters and seeds; a fully controlled one-factor comparison remains a camera-ready item and a natural first experiment for any 2.0 work.

    
Meta-review 4
Formalise the space assumptions
What can the language model choose about state, action and observation spaces? How are programs with different state dimensionality compared in the score and in equation 9?
Clarified: the observation program picks its own shape; on a shape mismatch the distance returns infinity and the candidate receives zero likelihood. The committee applies every transition program to shared belief particles, so vote entropy compares outputs on the same inputs.

    
Meta-review 5
Scope the claims
The setup assumes a known symbolic scaffold, a MiniGrid-specific distance and rich descriptions; "observation-only POMDP learning" overstates it.
Accepted in framing. The honest claim: an LLM can repair symbolic POMDP programs without realised state labels given a code API and a description. The no-description and semantic ablations quantify how much of the description is load-bearing.

    
Meta-review 6
Compute cost and API variance
How does filtering and scoring scale with environment size, and how much variance do API calls induce?
Added: per-run LLM calls, tokens, dollars and wall-clock per suite; a distractor-count scaling sweep on MiniGrid; and a fifty-fold replay of one identical request (36% ± 13 pp exact).

    
Meta-review 7
Clarify the oracle
An oracle planner with the true model was requested; a ground-truth reference already appeared in Figure 3.
Split into two references: "GT models" runs hand-written programs through the paper's own filter and planner (matchable); "Optimal" sees the state and plans exactly (a bound).

    
Authors
Fixed parts the prior could also optimise
The observation distance, the planner and the demonstration buffer are hand-designed and fixed. The same language model prior could search over them.

    
Still open
Selection cannot arbitrate facts the demonstrations lack
In the wrap-around seeds the repaired candidates were exact, yet the selector sometimes kept the old program: a likelihood computed on demonstrations cannot decide a question the demonstrations never ask. This is the precise gap that active induction, direction 1 below, targets.

14 · Sequencing

  
Four papers, in order

  

    
Paper A · ICLR 2027 delta or immediate follow-up
Closed-loop active induction and repair of executable world models
Directions 1 and 2, with 8 as a planner bonus, on the existing symbolic architecture. Smallest risk, answers the rigour-hungry reviewer, every experiment runs on current infrastructure. Headline metric: environment interactions until the model posterior concentrates above 0.9 on a correct program.

    
Paper B · efficiency
Amortised belief inference and a batched interpreter
Direction 4 with a JAX rewrite of the per-model filter. Independent of A, publishable at a systems-friendly venue; the FP8 recognition network is a legitimate contribution on its own.

    
Paper C · thesis centrepiece
Autotelic mapping of a model class's capabilities and vulnerabilities
Direction 9 with the library of direction 6 on a procedural task family. Reuses A's information-gain machinery; produces the developmental trajectories and the capability-vulnerability map the thesis is named after.

    
Paper D · the ambitious one
Structural prior plus neural latent dynamics
Direction 5, once A's active data collection exists to feed it. Direction 10's compiler experiment then falls out as the capstone ablation across A to D.

  

  
Three disciplines. Never combine more than two directions per submission. Keep the executable, verifiable story visible in every paper; it is the identity of the line of work. Verify any external analysis against the repository before building on it: the working document's notation section lists Tiger, RockSample, POPGym and ALFWorld as the benchmarks under discussion, while the released code evaluates MiniGrid, Blocks-rand6 and PopGym MineSweeper.
